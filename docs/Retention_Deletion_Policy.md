# Proposed retention and deletion policy

**Status: proposed defaults for TVVC owner review, 2026-10-16. Not approved or enforced. No data deletion is authorized by this document.**

This proposal covers the separate TVVC pilot's database, private media, backups and operator copies. It does not authorize cleanup of the local or hosted review environment, change existing backup schedules, or enable payments, email, public fundraising or real athlete/donor collection. All rehearsal data remains fictional. Any future Century/school use needs its own approved records requirements.

## Recommended defaults

Periods below are operational recommendations, not claims about statutory minimums. Use actual timestamps, not the simulator's clock. “Campaign close” means the recorded close event, not merely its scheduled end date. A campaign left open needs owner review rather than an indefinitely deferred retention clock; review open campaigns quarterly. Holds apply to the specific records needed, not automatically every contact in a campaign.

| Data | Proposed retention / action | Exceptions and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full encrypted database-plus-media recovery files, daily and manual | Rolling **30 days from creation**, including downloaded copies. No long-lived weekly/monthly full snapshots by default. | Before pruning, require at least two recent verified, complete recovery files and a successful isolated restore rehearsal within the last 90 days. If safety checks fail, stop pruning, flag the retention overrun and require owner review; do not silently extend retention forever. |
| Donation ledger, reconciliation evidence and financial audit | **7 years after the later of the relevant fiscal-year close or tax-return filing date**; owner/accountant confirms the applicable schedule. Preserve amounts, statuses, fees, dates, transaction/reference identifiers, corrections and only necessary payer/receipt evidence. | This is a conservative business default, not a universal IRS requirement. Unresolved payments, refunds, disputes or documented holds block removal of related evidence. Never keep the whole contact list or image library as a financial archive. |
| Outreach contacts, athlete-contact associations and message/job content | **90 days after campaign close**. Delete outreach addresses, personal text and capability-bearing bodies when no longer needed; retain only minimal completion/replay evidence. | A specific unresolved transaction can retain the minimum contact/evidence it needs until resolution plus 90 days. Unrelated contacts remain eligible. Do not copy contacts automatically into the next season. Suppression evidence follows its separate rule below. |
| Athlete profile names, photos and personal messages | End public visibility at campaign close; delete personal profile content and associated private images **90 days after close**. Unassigned staged images: **30 days after upload**. Hidden images: **30 days after hiding**, unless another authorized active use requires them. | On verified withdrawal, revoke visibility/access promptly and target source deletion within **30 days**, subject to a specific documented hold. Check all references before removing a shared asset. Preserve only pseudonymous IDs needed for historical financial attribution. |
| Athlete/guardian authorization evidence | Minimal dated scope, reviewer, evidence reference and withdrawal history: **3 years after last authorized use**, subject to organization approval. | Keep restricted evidence separate from public profiles. Avoid retaining identity documents or an extra copy of the photo. Apply the same schedule to the referenced evidence, not just its database pointer. Any longer required period needs a recorded reason. |
| Administrator accounts | Revoke access immediately on departure; remove email, password hash and unnecessary profile fields **90 days after deactivation**. | Keep a non-login actor ID for retained audits. Never remove the last owner without a verified successor. Existing foreign keys require a deliberate archival migration, not a cascading delete. |
| Security and operational activity | **90 days** for non-financial activity/security records; backup success/failure receipts **90 days** with no embedded data payloads. | Keep financial audit under its financial schedule. Deletion/hold approval records: **7 years from action**, using IDs, counts, reasons and outcomes rather than copies of erased content. Provider-managed logs need a separately verified schedule. |
| Temporary exports and decrypted restore workspaces | Remove **7 days after the task/rehearsal finishes**. | Inventory operator machines and downloads. A deliberately retained financial archive follows the financial schedule and remains encrypted and access restricted. Review/test artifacts need separately approved cleanup; this proposal does not remove them. |

### Access credentials, suppression and duplicate processing

- Keep the existing **8-hour session expiry** and **90-day athlete invitation expiry**. Revoke invitations at close or withdrawal. Proposed housekeeping removes expired/revoked credential material within 24 hours where safe; it does not grant access for an extra day. Current expiry/revocation behavior remains unchanged by this document.
- Keep organization-scoped unsubscribe/complaint/hard-bounce suppression while that organization may send fundraising messages. Do not expire an opt-out merely because a campaign ends or a contact is deleted. A future migration should replace raw addresses where feasible with a versioned, organization-scoped keyed digest of the normalized address plus reason/date. The digest remains sensitive, requires separate protected key custody, and must survive restores. It is not permission to send. Organization closure requires an explicit suppression disposal/transfer decision.
- Keep minimal payment-event, transaction-uniqueness and request replay records for the financial retention period. For requests without financial effects, retain minimal replay tombstones for the lifetime of the request namespace; retiring that namespace must make old retries fail closed. Deleting an asset or a response payload must not free an old request ID to create it again.
- After 90 days following terminal resolution, remove unnecessary cached response bodies, private receipt/link capabilities and personal request payloads only after replacing them with sufficient immutable fingerprint/resource/outcome evidence. Pending payment capabilities need an explicit reconciliation/access decision first. Current immutable records cannot simply be edited through the normal store.
- **Pending external payments remain pending, with solicitation held indefinitely until an administrator resolves them.** Age, retention eligibility, token expiry and campaign close must never confirm, reject, refund or resume solicitation automatically. A confirmed donation must not regain solicitation eligibility through contact deletion or restoration.

## Deletion requests and holds

The organization owner is accountable for decisions; administrators can help verify and inventory a request. Verify the requester and their authority through an established channel without collecting unnecessary new identity documents. Target acknowledgement within **7 days** and a decision/action within **30 days**; these are proposed service targets, not a statement of applicable legal deadlines. Any applicable shorter deadline takes precedence.

Separate immediate access/publication revocation from source deletion and eventual backup expiry. Explain any financial, dispute or legal exception narrowly: record IDs, reason, approver, start date and next review date. Review holds at least every 90 days; a review date never silently releases a hold or resolves a payment. Preserve relevant material for a documented obligation, then reassess when it ends. Do not retain an unrelated athlete photo because a donation is pending.

For organization closure, revoke access and publication promptly, export the minimum required financial records to an identified custodian, and propose deletion of other source data within 90 days. Outstanding transactions/holds and suppression handling need explicit decisions. Closure requires its own reviewed plan; it is not an automatic cascading delete.

## Backups, media and restore safety

Today, hiding an image only revokes library access. Its encrypted bytes remain stored and are included in subsequent backups. Full recovery files also contain administrator password hashes, contacts and private capabilities; sessions and login lockouts are excluded. Encryption does not substitute for retention limits.

Once deletion is approved and implemented, purge the source object and unnecessary source data together with a consistent metadata/tombstone update. New backups must omit erased bytes without failing because a retained metadata row still expects an image. Inventory upload-orphan objects as well as active/hidden assets; orphan eligibility requires a grace period, reference checks and approval.

Under the proposed 30-day recovery window, historical copies ordinarily expire within **30 days after source deletion**, not 30 days after initial collection or a hide action. For example, a source photo deleted November 1 can still exist in an October 31 recovery file until that file expires November 30. A safety/hold exception must be recorded and communicated; do not promise complete erasure while such copies remain.

Keep a restricted deletion/revocation journal outside the normal restorable snapshot history, using organization IDs, record IDs, effective dates and outcomes, not erased content. Before a recovered database is allowed to serve requests or make new backups, apply that journal and current suppressions, invalidate sessions, preserve account revocations and remove withdrawn content from both database and restored media. Restoring an old backup must not restore permission to publish, contact or sign in. Rehearse this in an isolated destination before enabling deletion.

Inventory cloud files, verification receipts, local encrypted downloads, legacy database-only backups, migration archives, decrypted recovery databases, exports and operator copies. Provider-managed database recovery, logs and storage-layer copies may have separate retention rules: verify those with Turso/Netlify before promising an erasure deadline. Record what was actually removed and any remaining copy/expiry. Do not claim physical-media overwrite from an application delete API.

The current master recovery key derives keys for all media and backups. Destroying it would affect the entire pilot; it is **not** a way to erase one person's data. Preserve recovery-key custody. Per-object cryptographic erasure would require a separate design.

## Safe implementation sequence after policy approval

1. Record the owner's approved periods, effective date, fiscal-year settings, accountable custodian and any accountant/organization requirements. Inventory existing records; missing timestamps or uncertain ownership must be reported as “needs review,” not guessed eligible.
2. Build an owner-only **read-only retention report** with per-category counts/bytes, eligibility dates, holds and dependency reasons. Keep donor addresses, private links and image content out of reports/logs. Scope the report to the pilot and verify server-side organization authorization. It must not mutate data or prune backups.
3. Design the dedicated archival/deletion migration. The current store rejects core/contact-association deletion; ledger/admin audit tables have append-only protections. Preserve financial integrity, actor/attribution stubs, uniqueness, suppression and replay protection. Do not bypass protections with broad deletes or foreign-key cascades. Move any personal audit content into an explicitly reviewed minimization plan rather than silently rewriting financial history.
4. Rehearse exact batches on an isolated copy: eligibility boundaries, scoped holds, duplicate retries/events, pending-payment holds, shared media, failed/partially completed deletion, resume/idempotency, backup creation after purge and restoration without resurrection. Retain an encrypted pre-change recovery file only within its approved retention/hold window.
5. Present a concrete batch for owner approval: IDs/counts, reasons, effects, backups that will expire, exceptions and recovery checks. Use reauthentication, a stable batch ID and resumable audited steps. Stop on changed preconditions. **No deletion or automatic cleanup until separately authorized.**
6. Only after successful rehearsal and approved execution, consider scheduled enforcement and independent failure/overrun monitoring. It must retain the current payments/email gates and leave the review environment untouched.

## Basis and remaining decisions

The [FTC's business data-security guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business) supports inventorying copies, minimizing collection, and a written retention/disposal policy. It does not prescribe the operational periods above. The [IRS recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records) describes different periods for different circumstances; the proposed seven-year financial default needs accountant confirmation. Applicable privacy, minor/guardian, school and email-provider requirements still need review before real collection or launch.

Next recommended engineering step: implement the read-only inventory/report after the owner reviews these defaults. This document does not implement that report, a financial archive, deletion journal, retention migration or cleanup worker. Existing data, backup retention and access behavior remain unchanged.
